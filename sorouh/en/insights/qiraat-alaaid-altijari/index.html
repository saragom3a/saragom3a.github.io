How to read the net yield on commercial assets without being misled by the numbers

The net yield quoted in many property offers is calculated in different ways. This article sets out what you should ask before accepting any figure.

This is an English translation of an article first published in Arabic. If the two versions differ, the Arabic original prevails. Read the Arabic original

In this article

  1. 01The figure alone is not enough
  2. 02Questions to ask before accepting the figure
  3. 03The typical gap
  4. 04The standard we hold ourselves to

The figure alone is not enough

When you are offered an asset at a net yield of eight per cent, you are looking at a figure that means nothing until you know how it was calculated. Both the numerator and the denominator of this equation can be shaped in many ways, and most of them are not fraud but differences in methodology.

Questions to ask before accepting the figure

  • Is the income used actual or projected? If it is projected, what occupancy rate is it based on?
  • Have operating expenses been deducted in full, or does the figure represent the gross yield rather than the net?
  • Has the vacancy period between tenants been accounted for, or has continuous full occupancy been assumed?
  • Is the cost of periodic capital maintenance included in the calculation?
  • Is the denominator the purchase price alone, or does it include acquisition and fit-out costs?

The typical gap

In our practical experience, the difference between the quoted yield and the yield actually achieved on commercial assets is often between one and two percentage points. This gap is not necessarily a calculation error; it is the result of optimistic assumptions piling up, each of which seems reasonable on its own.

The standard we hold ourselves to

For every opportunity we present, we show the assumptions the yield is built on, not just the yield. An investor who cannot see the assumptions cannot assess the figure, and so cannot make a decision.
