Why highest and best use must come before any development decision

Many development decisions start from what the owner wants rather than from a reading of the site. This article explains why that reversed order costs more than it seems.

This is an English translation of an article first published in Arabic. If the two versions differ, the Arabic original prevails. Read the Arabic original

In this article

  1. 01The problem starts with the wrong question
  2. 02What highest and best use actually means
  3. 03Examples of the reversed order
  4. 04Conclusion

The problem starts with the wrong question

The question landowners usually ask is: what should I build on my land? That question already assumes that building is the answer. The right question is: what does this site deserve to be?

The difference between the two questions is not a matter of wording. The first ends in a building; the second ends in a decision.

What highest and best use actually means

Determining the highest and best use is not a matter of picking from a list of architectural options. It is a methodical process of elimination built on four successive tests: legal permissibility, physical possibility, financial feasibility and maximum productivity.

The use that passes all four tests is the highest and best use. In many cases it turns out to be entirely different from what the owner expected.

Examples of the reversed order

  • A plot on a commercial street is developed as an office building because the owner prefers corporate leases, while the surrounding demand points to commercial use with a higher return.
  • A large plot is developed in one go, when phased development would have spread the risk and allowed the course to be corrected.
  • An existing asset is demolished and rebuilt, when refurbishment would have achieved a comparable return at a quarter of the cost.

Conclusion

The cost of a highest-and-best-use study does not compare with the cost of a wrong development decision. It is not an analytical luxury but risk management at the most sensitive stage of a project: the stage that cannot be undone once the foundations are poured.
